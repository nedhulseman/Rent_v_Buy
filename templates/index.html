Rent vs. Buy

A long-horizon look at what your money does in each scenario.

All of the factors considered ▾

Renting

  • Rent — steps up once a year at your rent-growth rate.
  • Full capital stays invested — the renter starts with the whole amount, since no down payment or closing costs are taken out.
  • Investment growth — compounded monthly at your expected return.
  • The monthly difference — in any month renting is cheaper, the saving is added to the renter's investments.

Not counted

  • Security deposits and renter's insurance
  • Cost of moving between rentals
  • Tax on investment gains

Owning

  • Mortgage — fixed payment, split into interest and principal on a real amortization schedule.
  • Down payment + closing, moving and realtor fees — taken out of starting capital, so the money you can't invest is charged against buying.
  • Property tax — annual rate on the current home value.
  • HOA / monthly fees
  • Maintenance — annual rate on current value (~1% rule of thumb).
  • Homeowners insurance — annual rate on current value.
  • PMI — charged until the balance falls below 80% of the purchase price, then drops off automatically.
  • Home appreciation — compounded monthly.
  • Equity — down payment + principal paid + appreciation.
  • Selling costs — commission and seller closing netted off equity at every point, so "if I sold today" is always the honest number.
  • Mortgage interest + property tax deduction — counted only for the amount that exceeds the standard deduction, with property tax capped by SALT. For many households this is legitimately $0.
  • The monthly difference — in any month owning is cheaper, the saving is added to the buyer's investments.

Not counted

  • Inflation on HOA and fees
  • Capital-gains treatment on a primary residence
  • Rental income, refinancing, extra principal payments

Both sides are compared on the same budget: whichever option costs less in a given month, the difference is invested at your expected return. That's what makes the two totals comparable.

How this is calculated ▾

We project two parallel timelines, month by month, over the full loan horizon. In each timeline we track everything you own (home equity, invested cash) so you can compare apples to apples — not just monthly payments.

The high-level factors

  • Mortgage payment — fixed monthly P&I from the standard amortization formula.
  • Home value — compounds monthly at the appreciation rate.
  • Equity — down payment + cumulative principal paid + appreciation gains.
  • Property tax — annual rate × current home value, billed monthly.
  • HOA & fees — fixed monthly cost.
  • Rent — steps up once a year by the rent-growth rate.
  • Surplus cash flow — whichever path is cheaper in a given month, the difference is invested at the expected return.
  • Buying total — home equity + side investments.
  • Renting total — full available capital invested from day one, plus the monthly surpluses.

The formulas

Mortgage payment = L × (r(1+r)n) / ((1+r)n − 1)

Home value(t) = Price × (1 + a/12)t

Rent(year y) = Rent₀ × (1 + g)y

Investments(t) = Investments(t−1) × (1 + i/12) + monthly surplus

L = loan, r = monthly mortgage rate, n = total payments, a = annual home appreciation, g = annual rent growth, i = annual investment return.

Now modelled

  • Maintenance and homeowners insurance, as annual rates on the current home value.
  • PMI, charged until the balance falls below 80% of the purchase price.
  • Selling costs, netted off equity at every period — so the crossover means "if I sold that month", not just at the end.
  • Mortgage interest + property tax deduction, counting only the amount that exceeds the standard deduction, with property tax capped by SALT. For many households after 2017 this is legitimately zero.

What this still doesn't model

  • Inflation on HOA / fees, and on the standard deduction.
  • Tax on investment returns (flatters the renting side).
  • Capital-gains exclusion on a primary residence (flatters nobody here — no gains tax is charged on either side).
  • Rent-side costs: deposits, renter's insurance, moving between rentals.
